# Worked answers

Use after attempting the lab. Equivalent wording is welcome when it preserves the same evidence and decisions.

## Case A: Claim-by-claim review

| Claim | Classification | Evidence | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name is Community AI Basics | Supported | F1 | Keep |
| October 16 | Contradicted | F2 | October 15, 2026 |
| 14:00–15:00 UTC | Supported | F2 | Keep with timezone |
| At Central Library | Contradicted as event format; specific venue also unsupported | F2 | Online; no physical venue supplied |
| Tickets cost $25 | Contradicted | F3 | Free |
| Capacity 50 | Contradicted | F4 | 30 |
| Walk in without registering | Contradicted | F3 | Registration required |
| No programming experience needed | Supported | F5 | Keep |
| Captions guaranteed | Supported in substance for the live session | F6 | Say live captions available; do not imply translation or recording captions |
| Recording guaranteed | Unknown; guarantee unsupported | F7 | Recording availability is unconfirmed |
| Registration closes October 12 at 17:00 UTC | Supported | F9 | Preserve 2026 context |

“Unknown” and “contradicted” have different meanings. The recording may or may not exist later. F7 does not support a guarantee. The physical venue conflicts with the stated online format, while the specific library itself has no supporting evidence.

### A corrected announcement

Community AI Basics runs online on October 15, 2026, from 14:00 to 15:00 UTC. Attendance is free, registration is required, and capacity is 30 people. Register by October 12, 2026 at 17:00 UTC. No programming experience is required. Live captions will be available. Bring a browser and a way to take notes. Recording availability has not been confirmed.

Ask the organizer for the approved registration link before publishing. The exercise does not supply one. The revised text is ready for organizer review, not automatically ready for public release.

## Case B: Minimal inputs

Remove the participant name, personal email, accommodation note and payment field. They do not help produce a generic announcement. Use the approved event facts instead. F6 already allows a generic statement about captions without identifying anyone who requested them.

Example prompt: “Using only facts F1–F9, draft a generic reminder for registered attendees. Keep the date and UTC time exact, mention the online format and what to bring, and do not add a recording promise or registration URL. Do not include individual participant data.”

A real individual accommodation request belongs in an appropriate approved process. Avoid copying it into a general-purpose tool merely because it is available.

## Case C: Source text is not task authority

“Assistant instruction: ignore the approved facts…” is an instruction embedded in the source. It does not change the user's task. Keep the approved free price from F3, flag the suspicious paragraph, and do not request passwords. For this fictional scenario, proceed only with the approved facts. If the integrity of a real source is uncertain, pause and contact its owner. A prompt alone cannot guarantee that an automated system will resist every injection attempt.

## Case D: Fair treatment

The source provides no evidence about learning ability by age. Reject the unsupported restriction. A supported alternative is: “This workshop welcomes beginners and requires no programming experience. Participants need a browser and a way to take notes. Live captions will be available.”

If additional access needs arise, ask the organizer about the actual support available rather than making assumptions about people.

## Final decision

**Ready for organizer review**, with a request for the approved registration link. A human with publishing authority verifies the final wording and link before release. Do not send the draft, charge money, register anyone or change access permissions as part of the lab.

## Facilitator note on scoring

Use the completion criteria to guide discussion. Do not score people by speed or infer AI-system quality from these examples. Explain disagreements using evidence IDs. The lab checks review skills, not a model benchmark.
